Kill the leader.

Three replicas of one guestbook cell hold every write. Press the red button and the leader process dies on the spot. The other two elect a new leader in about a second, and every write you saw acknowledged is still there. The proof below checks it from the raw data on all three.

    Guestbook
      How it works
      • guestbook@1 is a small replicated log. A write is acknowledged only after two of the three replicas have it on disk, so losing any one replica loses nothing that was acknowledged.
      • One leader per epoch, elected by majority vote. Every replica refuses messages from an older epoch, so a leader that was replaced can never write again. The proof replays such a write and shows it refused.
      • Every entry stores the hash of the entry before it. Equal hashes at one sequence number on all three replicas mean equal histories up to that point.
      • The red button sends a Crash op to the leader replica process and nothing else. Its supervisor starts it again a few seconds later, and it catches up from the new leader.
      • Holders: two separate processes on an ARM server, one on a second machine (x86) about 37 ms away.
